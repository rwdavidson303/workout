Upper-Body Recomposition Program

Build the Muscle That Makes You Look Lean — Four Sessions, Four Equipment Tiers, Anywhere in the World

Schedule: 4 days / week (M/W/F + Sat)
Duration: 30-55 min
Focus: Upper body, back-led
Equipment: Full gym down to nothing

The Goal: Build the Muscle, Not Shrink the Man

At 5'9¾" and 155 lbs there is very little fat sitting on top of anything. The definition you want comes from adding upper-body muscle, not from subtracting bodyweight. Cut from here and you get smaller and flatter, not sharper.

1

Eat at Maintenance, Not in a Deficit

A deficit is the right tool when there is fat to remove. When there is not, it removes muscle instead and the mirror gets worse, not better. Eat at maintenance with protein high and let training drive the change. The scale should sit flat or drift slightly up. (Helms et al., 2014; Slater et al., 2019)

2

Recomposition Is Genuinely Available to You

Gaining muscle and losing a little fat simultaneously is possible in three situations: new to training, returning after a long layoff, or well above average body fat. Coming back from a long layoff puts you squarely in the second. That window does not stay open forever, which is the argument for using it now. (Barakat et al., 2020)

3

Back Width Is the Whole Illusion

The "toned" look is a ratio, not a bodyweight. Wider shoulders and a wider back make the same waist read as smaller. That is why this program is back-led and why every session has a vertical or overhead pulling movement, which the previous program had none of anywhere.

4

Fewer, Harder Sets

The old program ran 163 sets a week across six days. This one runs 93 across four. Every set here is taken to within 1-2 reps of failure. Junk volume does not add stimulus; it just spends recovery you could have spent on something that worked.

Four Equipment Tiers, Same Four Sessions

Every session is written four ways. Look at where you are that morning and pick the tier — the slot in the week does not change

TierWhat You NeedHow It Differs
Dumbbells + Bench Home or hotel gym The default. Incline and flat pressing at full range, chest-supported rows, pullovers.
Dumbbells, No Bench Dumbbells only Floor presses and floor flyes replace bench work. Slightly shorter range at the bottom, same stimulus.
Bands Only Band set + door anchor The travel tier that actually trains your back. Banded pulldowns and face pulls are the only real vertical pulling available in a hotel room.
No Equipment A bed, a door, a towel Decline and pike push-ups carry the pressing. Door-handle rows and sliding pullovers carry the back.

Log the tier you used. The tracker keeps each tier as its own workout type, so a band press PR never gets confused with a dumbbell press PR.

The Exercise Pool

Weighted toward pulling, because pulling was the hole

GroupExercisesWhy They're In
Vertical Pull DB Pullover, Band Lat Pulldown, Sliding Lat Pullover The width that makes the waist look smaller. Absent entirely from the old program, and the single biggest change here.
Horizontal Pull Chest-Supported Row, One-Arm DB Row, Bent-Over Two-DB Row, Band Seated Row, Door-Handle Row, Towel Row Mid-back thickness and rear-delt balance against all the pressing.
Push Incline DB Press, Flat DB Press, DB Floor Press, Chest Fly, Overhead Press, Arnold Press, Decline Push-Up, Pike Push-Up Chest and front delts. Incline leads because upper chest is what reads through a shirt.
Delts & Arms Lateral Raise, Rear Delt Fly, Band Face Pull, Prone Y and T Raise, Skull Crusher, OH Tricep Extension, Diamond Push-Up, Incline Curl, Hammer Curl Side delts widen the frame, rear delts hold the posture, triceps are two thirds of the arm.
Core Weighted Crunch, Lying Leg Raise, Side Plank, Hollow Body Hold Nine loaded sets a week, three per upper session. Abs are a muscle, so train them like one and stop doing hundreds of unloaded reps.
Legs Bulgarian Split Squat, DB Romanian Deadlift, Reverse Lunge, Calf Raise, Glute Bridge, Wall Sit One 30-minute session. A deliberate maintenance dose, and this time the volume table says so honestly.
Carries Farmer's Walk, Suitcase Carry Grip, traps, and bracing. Cheap to recover from and they make everything else feel easier.

Form cues and tutorials for every movement, including all band and bodyweight variations, are on the Exercises page.

The Four Sessions

Three upper-body sessions and one short leg session. That is the whole week

Monday

A — Push-Lead Upper

The heavy day, and the one that actually builds the muscle. Never the session you skip.

  • 6-10 reps, 3-4 sets per exercise
  • 90-120 sec rest between sets
  • Chest 7, back 7, delts 3, arms 6
  • 3 loaded core sets + farmer's walk
  • ~50-55 min
View Workout
Wednesday

B — Pull-Lead Upper

The back day. Vertical pull first, then rows, then rear delts. This is the session that builds the V.

  • 10-15 reps, 3-4 sets per exercise
  • 60-90 sec rest between sets
  • Back 10, chest 3, delts 6, arms 6
  • 3 loaded core sets
  • ~50 min
View Workout
Friday

C — Detail Upper

Chest, delts and arms at higher reps. The finishing coat on what Monday and Wednesday built.

  • 10-15 reps, 3 sets per exercise
  • 60-75 sec rest between sets
  • Chest 6, back 6, delts 6, arms 6
  • 3 loaded core sets
  • ~45-50 min
View Workout
Saturday

D — Legs + Carries

Half an hour to hold what you have. The one session to drop when the week goes sideways.

  • 10-15 reps, 3 sets per exercise
  • 60-90 sec rest between sets
  • 9 leg sets + calves + carries
  • Explicitly a maintenance dose
  • ~30 min
View Workout

Tuesday, Thursday, Sunday: walk. 8-10k steps a day plus two 25-minute easy walks a week. Walking buys the same calories a circuit day does and costs almost nothing in recovery, which is the entire reason the old Friday circuit session is gone.
