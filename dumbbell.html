Upper-Body Recomposition Program

Build the Muscle That Makes You Look Lean — Four Sessions, Five Equipment Tiers, Anywhere in the World

Schedule: 4 days / week (M/W/F + Sat)
Duration: 55-100 min
Focus: Upper body, back-led
Equipment: Bench, dumbbells, kettlebells, bands, or nothing

The Goal: Build the Muscle, Not Shrink the Man

At 5'9¾" and 155 lbs there is very little fat sitting on top of anything. The definition you want comes from adding upper-body muscle, not from subtracting bodyweight. Cut from here and you get smaller and flatter, not sharper.

1

Eat at Maintenance, Not in a Deficit

A deficit is the right tool when there is fat to remove. When there is not, it removes muscle instead and the mirror gets worse, not better. Eat at maintenance with protein high and let training drive the change. The scale should sit flat or drift slightly up. (Helms et al., 2014; Slater et al., 2019)

2

Recomposition Is Genuinely Available to You

Gaining muscle and losing a little fat simultaneously is possible in three situations: new to training, returning after a long layoff, or well above average body fat. Coming back from a long layoff puts you squarely in the second. That window does not stay open forever, which is the argument for using it now. (Barakat et al., 2020)

3

Back Width Is the Whole Illusion

The "toned" look is a ratio, not a bodyweight. Wider shoulders and a wider back make the same waist read as smaller. That is why this program is back-led and why every session has a vertical or overhead pulling movement, which the previous program had none of anywhere.

4

Effort Is Prescribed, Reps Are Not

You train in gyms where the dumbbells change week to week. A fixed "4 x 6-8" means you stop at 8 reps with a weight you could have pushed to 20, and the set never gets near failure. So every exercise here lists a rep window and a target reps-in-reserve. Take the heaviest pair on the rack, stop at the listed RIR, and let the rep count land where it lands. Hypertrophy tracks proximity to failure with no clean threshold (Robinson et al., 2024).

Five Equipment Tiers, Same Four Sessions

Every session is written five ways. Look at what the gym has that morning and pick the tier — the slot in the week does not change

TierWhat You NeedHow It Differs
Dumbbells + Bench Home or hotel gym The default. Incline and flat pressing at full range, chest-supported rows, pullovers.
Dumbbells, No Bench Dumbbells only Floor presses and floor flyes replace bench work. Slightly shorter range at the bottom, same stimulus.
Kettlebells One or two bells Floor presses, rows, high pulls and swings. Load jumps between bells are huge, so tempo and pauses bridge the gap.
Bands Only Band set + door anchor The travel tier that actually trains your back. Banded pulldowns and face pulls are the only real vertical pulling available in a hotel room.
No Equipment A bed, a door, a towel Decline and pike push-ups carry the pressing. Door-handle rows and sliding pullovers carry the back.

Log the tier you used. The tracker keeps each tier as its own workout type, so a band press PR never gets confused with a dumbbell press PR.

The Exercise Pool

Every muscle gets two exercises in every session, paired stretched-then-contracted

GroupExercisesWhy They're In
Vertical Pull DB Pullover, Band Lat Pulldown, Sliding Lat Pullover (towel), One-Arm DB Pullover, One-Arm Band Pulldown, One-Arm Sliding Pullover The width that makes the waist look smaller. Absent entirely from the pre-v2 program.
Horizontal Pull Chest-Supported Row, Band Seated Row, Door-Handle Row, One-Arm DB Row, Band One-Arm Row, Towel Row (one arm), Wide-Elbow Bench Row, Band Wide Row (elbows high), Wide-Grip Door Row Mid-back thickness and rear-delt balance against all the pressing.
Chest Incline DB Press, Band Incline Press, Decline Push-Up (feet on bed), Flat DB Bench Press, Band Chest Press, Push-Up, DB Chest Fly, Band Chest Fly, Wide Push-Up… Upper, mid and stretched-position work. Incline leads because upper chest is what reads through a shirt.
Shoulders Seated Overhead Press, Band Overhead Press, Pike Push-Up (feet on bed), Arnold Press, Band Arnold Press, Elevated Pike Push-Up, Half-Kneeling One-Arm Press, Half-Kneeling Band Press, Assisted One-Arm Pike Push-Up… All three heads every session: a press for the front, a raise for the side, a fly or row for the rear.
Traps DB Shrug, Band Shrug, Loaded Bag Shrug, DB High Pull, Band High Pull, Prone W Raise, Prone Y Raise (incline bench), Band Y Raise, Prone Y Raise… New in v3. Shrugs build the upper fibres; high pulls and Y raises build the mid and lower ones that hold posture.
Triceps OH Tricep Extension, Band OH Tricep Extension, Sliding Tricep Extension (towel), Close-Grip DB Press, Band Close-Grip Press, Diamond Push-Up, Skull Crusher, Band Skull Crusher, Bodyweight Skull Crusher… Two thirds of the arm. Every session pairs an overhead movement for the long head with a heavy press for the lateral.
Biceps Incline Curl, Band Curl, Towel Curl (supinated), Hammer Curl, Band Hammer Curl, Towel Curl (neutral grip), Incline Hammer Curl, Staggered Band Hammer Curl, One-Arm Towel Hammer Curl… Stretched-position curls for the long head, neutral-grip work for the brachialis underneath it.
Forearms Seated Wrist Curl, Band Wrist Curl, Towel Wring (flexors), Reverse Curl, Band Reverse Curl, Towel Reverse Curl, Reverse Wrist Curl, Band Reverse Wrist Curl, Towel Wring (extensors) New in v3. Wrist curls for the flexors, reverse curls for the extensors and the brachioradialis.
Core & Carries Weighted Crunch, Band Crunch, Hollow Body Hold (30 sec), Lying Leg Raise, Side Plank (30 sec/side), Farmer's Walk (40 sec), Suitcase Carry (40 sec), Wall Sit (60 sec) Loaded sets, not hundreds of unloaded reps. Carries add grip, traps and bracing for free.
Legs Bulgarian Split Squat, Band Split Squat, Bulgarian Split Squat (BW), Goblet Squat, Band Goblet Squat, Sissy Squat, DB Romanian Deadlift, Band Romanian Deadlift, Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift… Each leg muscle gets a stretched movement and a contracted one, same rule as the upper days.

Form cues and tutorials for every movement, including all band and bodyweight variations, are on the Exercises page.

The Four Sessions

Three complete upper-body sessions and one leg session. Every muscle, every session

Monday

A · Upper Body

The heavy day. Every muscle, presses leading, biggest loads of the week. Never the session you skip.

  • 17 exercises, 52 working sets
  • 8-25 rep window, effort set by RIR
  • 2 min on the first four, 90 sec on everything after
  • chest 7, back 8, shoulders 10, traps 5, triceps 6, biceps 6, forearms 4, core 6
  • ~100 minutes
View Workout
Wednesday

B · Upper Body

The same muscles from different angles: one arm at a time on the back, rotated pressing, contracted-position curls.

  • 17 exercises, 51 working sets
  • 10-25 rep window, effort set by RIR
  • 2 min on the first four, 90 sec on everything after
  • chest 7, back 7, shoulders 10, traps 5, triceps 6, biceps 6, forearms 4, core 6
  • ~95 minutes
View Workout
Friday

C · Upper Body

The high-rep day, and the one a light rack cannot spoil. Angles the first two days did not cover.

  • 17 exercises, 52 working sets
  • 10-25 rep window, effort set by RIR
  • 2 min on the first four, 90 sec on everything after
  • chest 7, back 8, shoulders 10, traps 5, triceps 6, biceps 6, forearms 4, core 6
  • ~95 minutes
View Workout
Saturday

D · Legs + Calves

Quads, hamstrings, glutes and calves, two exercises each. The one session to drop when a week goes sideways.

  • 9 exercises, 29 working sets
  • 10-25 rep window, effort set by RIR
  • 2 min on the first four, 90 sec on everything after
  • quads 7, hamstrings 7, glutes 6, calves 6
  • ~55 minutes
View Workout

Tuesday, Thursday, Sunday: walk. 8-10k steps a day plus two 25-minute easy walks a week. Walking buys the same calories a circuit day does and costs almost nothing in recovery, which is the entire reason the old Friday circuit session is gone.

184 working sets a week across four days — 155 on the three upper sessions (17 exercises each) and 29 on legs (9 exercises). Every number on this page is counted from the actual sessions, never estimated. The total is larger than v2's 113 because v3 trains eight muscle groups directly instead of five: traps and forearms are now programmed rather than left to whatever the rows happened to give them. Per muscle the numbers still sit at the top of the productive range rather than past it, which is where the dose-response curve flattens without turning down (Pelland et al., 2025).
