Progression on a Cut

When you're eating in a deficit, the win is holding your numbers — not always adding weight

What "Progress" Means While Cutting

On a bulk you chase bigger lifts. On a cut, the scoreboard is different.

1

Maintaining Strength = Winning

In a calorie deficit you have less fuel and recover slower. If your weights and reps stay the same week to week while the scale drops, you are keeping muscle and losing fat. That is a successful cut, even with zero added load.

2

Push Load Where You Can

Early in the cut, and on your stronger lifts, you'll still add reps or weight using double progression (below). Take the gains when they come — just don't expect them on every lift every week.

3

Effort Is the Constant

Whatever the weight, every working set should finish 1-2 reps shy of failure. That proximity to failure is the real driver of keeping muscle — not the number on the dumbbell.

Double Progression (When You're Adding)

The cleanest way to push a dumbbell lift forward: add reps first, then weight

1

Start at the Bottom of the Range

Begin at a weight you can control for the lowest rep count of the day's range (e.g., Strength day = 6 reps).

2

Add Reps Over Weeks

Each week, add 1-2 reps per set at the same weight. When you hit the top of the range on all sets, you've earned the jump.

3

Add Weight, Reset Reps

Move up one increment (often 5 lb) and drop back to the bottom of the range. Repeat.

Day Type Rep Range Example Progression
Strength (M) 6 → 10 reps 30 lb x 6 → 30 lb x 8 → 30 lb x 10 → 35 lb x 6
Hypertrophy (W) 10 → 12 reps 20 lb x 10 → 20 lb x 11 → 20 lb x 12 → 25 lb x 10
Metabolic (F) 12 → 15 reps Beat last week's circuit time, or add 1-2 reps — not necessarily weight

When a Lift Goes Backward

A deeper cut will eventually pull some numbers down. Here's how to read it and respond.

What You See What It Means What To Do
A single off day Poor sleep, low fuel, or stress — normal noise in a deficit. Nothing. Hit the same target next session.
Reps slipping on one lift for 2-3 weeks That movement is fatigued, not necessarily losing muscle. Hold the weight, accept fewer reps, keep effort high. It rebounds.
Strength dropping across the board Recovery debt — the deficit and/or training is outrunning recovery. Take a deload week (below), check sleep and protein, and make sure the deficit isn't too aggressive.
Losing muscle look + strength fast Deficit is too steep and/or protein too low. Eat more protein and ease the deficit. Slower fat loss keeps more muscle — see Nutrition.

A Typical 8-12 Week Cut

Weeks 1-2: Set the Baseline

  • Learn every movement and lock in form, tempo, and the superset/circuit flow
  • Log the weight and reps you hit on each lift — this is the number you'll defend
  • Start the nutrition deficit moderate, not aggressive
  • Expect to feel strong — the deficit hasn't accumulated yet

Weeks 3-6: Hold & Sharpen

  • Defend your strength numbers — same weights, same reps, week to week
  • Tighten rest periods on Hypertrophy and Metabolic days to raise the burn
  • Add reps or load on the lifts that still feel strong; don't force the rest
  • Fat loss becomes visible — the core block starts to show

Weeks 7-12: Protect the Muscle

  • This is where strength wants to dip — keep effort high and accept flat or slightly lower reps
  • Keep protein at the top of its range; this is when it matters most
  • Prioritize the heavy Monday lifts — they are the muscle-retention insurance
  • Push the Friday conditioning if fat loss stalls before adding more diet restriction

Every 5-6 Weeks: Deload

  • Drop to 2 sets per exercise and reduce weight ~30% for one week
  • Keep all three day types, just lighter — this is recovery, not a week off
  • Consider eating at maintenance (no deficit) for that week to recharge
  • You'll come back stronger and protect more muscle for the rest of the cut

When You Can't Add Weight

With a limited dumbbell range you'll cap out. These keep a lift challenging without more load.

Strategy How It Works When to Use
Slow the Eccentric Move to a 3-4 sec lowering phase. More time under tension at the same weight. When you can hit the top of the range easily
Add Pauses 1-2 sec pause at the hardest point (bottom of a press, top of a row). Kills momentum. When a lift starts to feel easy
Go Unilateral Two-arm work → one-arm. Doubles the effective load per limb. When bilateral lifts are maxed out
Shorten Rest Same work in less time = more metabolic demand and calorie burn. Hypertrophy & Metabolic days — beat last week's clock
Add a Set Go from 3 to 4 sets on a lagging area. Volume drives both size and retention. When reps and weight are both maxed

Tempo Guide

Tempo notation: Eccentric — Pause — Concentric (in seconds)

Day Tempo Meaning Why
Strength 3-1-1 3 sec down, 1 sec pause, 1 sec up Control + tension to protect muscle at heavier loads
Hypertrophy 2-1-1 2 sec down, 1 sec pause at stretch, 1 sec up Maximizes the pump and mechanical tension
Metabolic 2-0-1 2 sec down, no pause, 1 sec up Keeps the circuit flowing and the heart rate high

Why the 3-Day Variation Works for a Cut

Day Stimulus Job on a Cut How It Feels
Strength (M) Heavy loads, longer rest, slow eccentrics Signals your body to keep its muscle in the deficit Heavy and deliberate — you should feel strong
Hypertrophy (W) Moderate loads, supersets, short rest Builds detail and burns calories at the same time Pumped and fatigued — muscles feel full
Metabolic (F) Lighter loads, circuits, minimal rest Maximizes calorie burn and EPOC for fat loss Breathless and sweating — heart rate stays up
