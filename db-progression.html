Progression

Progression on a Recomp

Eating at maintenance means the numbers should go up. If they are flat for a month, something is wrong.

What Progress Means Here

On a cut, holding your numbers is the win. On a recomp, holding is the warning sign

1

The Lifts Are the Scoreboard

You are eating enough to recover and enough to build. That means the load or the reps should climb on most lifts most weeks, especially early. If your incline press is the same in eight weeks as it is today, you did not do a recomp, you did maintenance.

2

The Scale Is Not the Scoreboard

Flat or drifting slightly up is exactly right. Muscle is denser than fat, so the mirror and the tape can improve substantially while the scale barely moves. Weigh weekly, average it, and ignore any single day.

3

Effort Is Still the Constant

Every working set finishes 1-2 reps short of failure. That proximity is what drives growth. Ninety-three hard sets beats a hundred and sixty-three vague ones, and it is the only reason the volume cut is safe.

4

Measure Four Things, Not One

Bodyweight weekly average, waist at the navel, the load on your top three lifts, and a monthly photo in the same light. Any three of those moving the right way is a working program regardless of what the fourth does.

Double Progression

The cleanest way to push a dumbbell lift forward: add reps first, then weight

1

Start at the Bottom of the Range

Begin at a weight you can control for the lowest rep count of that session's range. Session A starts at 6, sessions B and C start at 10 or 12.

2

Add Reps Over Weeks

Each week add 1-2 reps per set at the same weight. When you hit the top of the range on every set, you have earned the jump.

3

Add Weight, Reset Reps

Move up one increment, usually 5 lb, and drop back to the bottom of the range. Repeat. That is the whole method.

A Typical 12-Week Block

Weeks 1-2: Set the Baseline

  • Learn every movement and lock in tempo. Do not chase weight yet
  • Log the load and reps on every lift. These are the numbers you will beat
  • Run each tier at least once so you know what your hotel session actually looks like
  • Get intake to maintenance and protein to 170g before judging anything

Weeks 3-6: Add Load

  • This is where the numbers should move fastest. Take every jump on offer
  • Expect the fastest gains on pulling, since it is the least-trained pattern for you
  • Waist should hold steady while the shoulders and back start to change
  • Take the first monthly photo at week 4, same light, same time of day

Weeks 7-12: Grind the Gains

  • Progress slows and gets lift-specific. Some lifts climb, others sit. Both are normal
  • Switch the stalled lifts to tempo and pause progression rather than forcing load
  • Keep protein at 170g. It matters more, not less, as the easy gains dry up
  • Prioritise Monday above everything. It is the session doing the building

Every 6-8 Weeks: Deload

  • Drop to 2 sets per exercise and reduce load about 30% for one week
  • Keep all four sessions, just lighter. This is recovery, not a week off
  • Keep eating normally. Do not cut calories during a deload
  • You come back stronger, which is the entire point
