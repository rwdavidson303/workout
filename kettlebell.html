Kettlebell Training

Kettlebells Are Now an Equipment Tier, Not a Separate Program

Schedule: Same 4 sessions (M/W/F + Sat)
Duration: 30-60 min
Focus: Upper body, back-led
Equipment: One or two bells

What Changed, and Why

The old kettlebell program ran the same 13 exercises three times a week for 129 sets, on the same Monday, Wednesday and Friday the dumbbell program claims. Two full programs competing for the same three days is not a plan you can actually run, and 129 sets a week from one implement is far past the point where added volume pays for itself.

So kettlebells now work the way bands and bodyweight do: as a tier of the same four sessions. Same week, same effort targets, same volume, executed with bells. You pick the tier based on what the gym has that day, not on which program you feel like following.

  1. Same four sessions — A push-lead, B pull-lead, C detail, and Legs.
  2. Same effort rule — a rep window and a target reps-in-reserve, because bells jump in huge increments and a fixed rep target guarantees you finish nowhere near failure.
  3. 113 sets a week instead of 129 from bells alone, spread across every muscle rather than piled onto the same 13 movements.
  4. Swings and high pulls survive. Swings are the hip-extension movement in the Legs session; high pulls are the rear-delt movement in Sessions B and C.

Between two bells the load gap can be 8 kg or more. When the next bell up is too heavy, do not just add reps forever: slow the lowering to 4 seconds, add a 2-second pause at the stretch, then go one arm at a time. That ladder is how you bridge the gap.

What Was Cut, and Why

Judged against the same evidence base as the rest of the program

WasNowReason
Waist Halos & Head Halos Warm-up only 12 near-unloaded sets a week were being counted as training. They are good shoulder mobility. They are not a working set.
Push Outs, Side Extensions Dropped Low load and low return for 18 sets a week. That volume moved to triceps and rear delts, which were genuinely under-trained.
Same 13 exercises, 3×/week Three different sessions Once weekly volume is equated, extra frequency adds nothing measurable for muscle growth (Pelland et al., 2025). Repeating a session is not extra stimulus.
Standard / Density / Power Push / Pull / Detail The three old days trained the same movements at three tempos. The new three train different muscles, which is what actually distributes volume.
Fixed sets and reps Rep window + RIR With one bell you cannot adjust load at all, so effort is the only variable left to prescribe. Hypertrophy tracks proximity to failure (Robinson et al., 2024).
No horizontal pressing KB floor press added The old program had no chest movement at all. Floor press and deficit push-ups fix that.

The old Monday/Wednesday/Friday kettlebell program is still in the tracker under Kettlebell M/W/F (retired), so all of its history and personal records stay reachable. It is simply no longer part of the week.
