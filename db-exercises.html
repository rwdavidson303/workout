Dumbbell Exercise Library

Every movement in the cut program — mainstream lifts only, with form cues & tutorials

Primary Lifts (Illustrated Form Guides)

1

Bulgarian Split Squat

Compound Lower

Rear foot elevated on bench — lower until rear knee nearly touches floor

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Stand 2 feet in front of bench, place rear foot laces-down on bench
  • Hold dumbbells at sides, shoulders back and down
  • Lower straight down — front shin stays vertical
  • Rear knee drops toward floor, not forward
  • Drive through front heel to stand, squeeze glute at top
  • Keep torso upright — slight forward lean is OK
  • If balance is an issue, use lighter weights until stable
Used in: Optional alternative — swap for Reverse Lunge or Goblet Squat for variety
2

DB Romanian Deadlift

Compound Lower

Hinge at hips, slide DBs down thighs — stop when you feel hamstring stretch

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Stand with feet hip-width, soft bend in knees (10-15 degrees)
  • Push hips BACK, not down — this is a hinge, not a squat
  • DBs slide along the front of your thighs, staying close to body
  • Lower until you feel a strong hamstring stretch (usually mid-shin)
  • Keep back flat — neutral spine throughout
  • Drive hips forward to stand, squeeze glutes at top
  • Think "close the car door with your hips"
Used in: Strength (M) 3×10 — Hypertrophy (W) SS6b 3×12
3

DB Hip Thrust

Compound Lower

Upper back on bench, DB on hip crease — drive through heels, squeeze glutes at top

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Sit on floor with upper back against bench edge, knees bent, feet flat
  • Place DB on hip crease, hold in place with both hands
  • Drive through heels to extend hips until thighs are parallel to floor
  • Squeeze glutes hard at the top for 1-2 seconds
  • Lower hips under control — don't just drop down
  • Keep chin tucked slightly — look forward, not at ceiling
Used in: Optional glute accessory — add to any day for extra lower-body work
4

Reverse Lunge

Compound Lower

Step backward, not forward — less knee shear, more glute activation

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Stand tall with DBs at sides, step one foot BACKWARD
  • Lower until rear knee nearly touches floor (both knees at ~90 degrees)
  • Front shin stays vertical — knee doesn't shoot past toes
  • Drive through front heel to return to start
  • Alternate legs or complete all reps on one side, then switch
  • Keep torso upright throughout — core braced
Used in: Hypertrophy (W) SS6a 3×12/leg
5

Incline DB Press (30°)

Compound Push

Bench at 30° incline — 91% MVC for upper chest activation

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Set bench to 30 degrees (one or two notches from flat)
  • Feet flat on floor, shoulder blades pinched back and down
  • Start with DBs at chest height, palms forward
  • Press up and slightly together — don't clang them at top
  • Lower to deep stretch at chest level (deeper than barbell allows)
  • 1-second pause at bottom on strength day
  • Maintain natural arch in lower back — don't flatten
Used in: Strength (M) 4×6-8 — Hypertrophy (W) SS1a 3×12
6

One-Arm DB Row

Compound Pull

Hand and knee on bench — pull DB to hip, squeeze shoulder blade

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Place one hand and same-side knee on bench for support
  • Free arm hangs straight down holding DB, back flat like a table
  • Pull DB to your hip (not your armpit) — leads with elbow
  • Squeeze shoulder blade toward spine for 1 second at top
  • Lower under control — don't just drop the weight
  • Avoid rotating torso — hips and shoulders stay square
  • Think "start a lawnmower" motion
Used in: Strength (M) 4×8/arm — Hypertrophy (W) SS2b 3×12/arm — Metabolic (F) Circuit A 12/arm
7

Chest-Supported Row

Compound Pull

Face-down on incline bench (30-45°) — row DBs to ribcage, squeeze shoulder blades

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Set bench to 30-45 degrees, lie face-down with chest supported
  • Let arms hang straight down, holding DBs with neutral or pronated grip
  • Row DBs up to ribcage, leading with elbows
  • Squeeze shoulder blades together at the top for 1 second
  • Eliminates momentum — strict back activation only
  • Lower under control, feel the stretch at bottom
Used in: Strength (M) 3×10 — Hypertrophy (W) SS1b 3×12
8

Standing Overhead Press

Compound Push

Standing engages core as stabilizer — 79% MVC for shoulders

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Stand with feet shoulder-width, core braced tight
  • DBs start at shoulder height, palms facing forward
  • Press straight up to full lockout — biceps near ears
  • Don't arch back — squeeze glutes to prevent leaning
  • Lower under control back to shoulders
  • Breathe out on the press, in on the lower
Used in: Strength (M) seated 3×8 — see also Arnold Press & Thruster
9

Incline Curl

Isolation

Incline angle stretches long head — higher activation than standing curl

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Set bench to 45-55 degrees, sit back fully
  • Let arms hang straight down — feel the bicep stretch
  • Curl DBs up by bending elbows only — no swinging
  • Squeeze biceps hard at the top for 1 second
  • Lower slowly (3 sec on strength day) — this is where growth happens
  • Keep upper arms stationary — don't let elbows drift forward
Used in: Hypertrophy (W) SS4a 3×12
11

Overhead Tricep Extension

Isolation

One DB, both hands — lower behind head, extend to lockout

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Hold one DB with both hands (diamond grip on the top plate)
  • Start with DB overhead, arms fully extended
  • Lower behind head by bending elbows only (3-sec negative)
  • Keep elbows pointing forward — don't let them flare wide
  • Feel full tricep stretch at bottom, then extend back to lockout
  • Upper arms stay stationary — only forearms move
Used in: Metabolic (F) Circuit B 15 reps
13

Lateral Raise

Accessory

Raise to shoulder height only — peak medial deltoid activation

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Stand with slight forward lean (5-10 degrees)
  • DBs at sides, slight bend in elbows (locked throughout)
  • Raise arms out to sides, leading with elbows not hands
  • Stop at shoulder height — going higher risks impingement
  • Pause 1 second at top, lower under control (2 sec)
  • Think "pour water from a pitcher" at the top
  • Don't use momentum — if you're swinging, go lighter
Used in: Hypertrophy (W) SS5b 3×15 — Metabolic (F) Circuit B 15 reps
14

Bent-Over Rear Delt Fly

Accessory

85% MVC posterior deltoid — critical for shoulder balance and posture

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Hinge forward at hips to 45 degrees, flat back
  • DBs hang straight down, slight bend in elbows
  • Raise arms out to sides in a reverse fly motion
  • Squeeze shoulder blades together at top for 1 second
  • Lower under control — don't just drop the weights
  • Keep chest pointing at floor throughout
  • Think "spread your wings"
Used in: Hypertrophy (W) SS3b 3×15
15

Goblet Squat

Compound Lower

Quads, Glutes & Core

Hold one DB vertically at your chest — squat to full depth, chest tall

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Hold one DB by the top end, against your chest, elbows down
  • Feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out
  • Sit hips back and down, knees tracking over toes
  • Go to full depth — thighs at least parallel
  • Keep chest tall and core braced; the DB counterbalances you
  • Drive through mid-foot and heels to stand
Used in: Strength (M) leg primer 3×10 — Metabolic (F) Circuit A 15 reps

More Upper-Body Movements

16

Flat DB Bench Press

Compound Push

Mid Chest, Front Delts, Triceps

Flat bench — press both DBs from a deep chest stretch to lockout

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Lie flat, feet planted, shoulder blades pinched back and down
  • Start with DBs at chest level, palms facing forward
  • Press up and slightly together — don't clang them at the top
  • Lower to a deep stretch at chest level (deeper than a barbell allows)
  • Keep a natural arch in the lower back, wrists stacked over elbows
  • Control the lower — that's where the chest works hardest
Used in: Strength (M) 3×8-10
17

DB Chest Fly

Isolation

Chest (Stretch & Squeeze)

Wide arc with a soft elbow bend — open to a stretch, hug back together

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Lie on a flat or low-incline bench, DBs pressed above the chest, palms facing each other
  • Keep a fixed, soft bend in the elbows the entire time
  • Open the arms in a wide arc until you feel a deep chest stretch
  • Squeeze the chest to bring the DBs back together over the chest
  • Think "hugging a barrel" — the elbow angle never changes
  • Go lighter than you press; this is a stretch and squeeze, not a grind
Used in: Hypertrophy (W) SS2a 3×12
18

Arnold Press

Compound Push

All Three Shoulder Heads

Start palms-in at the chin, rotate to palms-forward as you press overhead

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Seated or standing, start with DBs at chin height, palms facing you
  • As you press up, rotate the palms to face forward
  • Finish at full lockout overhead, biceps near the ears
  • Reverse the rotation on the way down to the start
  • The rotation recruits front and side delts in one rep
  • Brace the core; don't lean back to cheat the weight up
Used in: Hypertrophy (W) SS3a 3×12 — Metabolic (F) Circuit B 12 reps
19

Push-Ups

Compound Push

Chest, Triceps, Core

Body in a straight line — lower the chest to the floor, press back up

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Hands slightly wider than shoulders, body in a straight line head to heels
  • Brace the core and squeeze the glutes — no sagging hips
  • Lower until the chest nearly touches the floor, elbows ~45° from the body
  • Press back to full lockout
  • Too easy? Elevate the feet. Too hard? Drop to the knees to keep moving
  • Full range every rep beats half-reps for show
Used in: Metabolic (F) Circuit A 15 reps
20

Skull Crusher (Lying Tricep Extension)

Isolation

Triceps (All Heads)

Lying down — lower DBs beside the head by bending only the elbows, extend to lockout

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Lie on a flat bench, press two DBs up over the chest, palms facing each other
  • Keep the upper arms vertical and still — only the forearms move
  • Bend the elbows to lower the DBs beside or just behind the head
  • Stop at a comfortable stretch, then extend back to lockout
  • Keep the elbows tucked — don't let them flare wide
  • Control the lower; this protects the elbows and builds the triceps
Used in: Strength (M) SS 3×10 — Hypertrophy (W) SS4b 3×12
21

Hammer Curl

Isolation

Biceps & Brachialis (Arm Thickness)

Neutral grip (palms facing each other) — curl without swinging

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Stand tall, DBs at sides, palms facing each other (thumbs up)
  • Keep that neutral grip the whole rep — no rotation
  • Curl by bending the elbows only; upper arms stay pinned to the sides
  • Squeeze at the top, lower under control
  • The neutral grip builds the brachialis, which pushes the biceps up and adds arm thickness
  • If you're swinging, go lighter
Used in: Strength (M) SS 3×10 — Hypertrophy (W) SS5a 3×12 — Metabolic (F) Circuit B 12 reps
22

DB Pullover

Compound Pull

Lats & Chest (Ribcage Expansion)

One DB held over the chest — lower it back over the head, pull it back

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Lie flat on a bench, hold one DB with both hands over the chest
  • Keep a slight, fixed bend in the elbows
  • Lower the DB in an arc back over and behind the head until you feel a lat/chest stretch
  • Pull it back over the chest using the lats and chest, not the arms
  • Keep the hips down and ribs from flaring — brace the core
  • A great upper-body stretch and a movement most people skip
Used in: Optional accessory — swap into Hypertrophy (W) for variety
23

Renegade Row

Compound Pull

Back + Core (Anti-Rotation)

Plank on two DBs — row one DB to the hip while bracing against rotation, alternate

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Start in a high plank gripping two DBs, hands under shoulders
  • Set the feet wider than normal for a stable base
  • Row one DB to the hip while keeping the hips square to the floor
  • Resist the urge to twist — the anti-rotation is the core work
  • Lower under control, then row the other side
  • Use moderate weight; stability comes before load here
Used in: Hypertrophy (W) finisher 10 total

Core & Abs

24

Weighted Crunch

Core

Upper Abs

DB held on the chest — curl the ribcage toward the hips, slow down

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat
  • Hold a DB against the chest or on the upper chest
  • Curl the shoulders up by shortening the abs — ribs toward hips
  • Don't pull on the neck; eyes to the ceiling, chin off the chest
  • Lower slowly — the negative builds the abs
  • Adding load is how the abs get thicker and show more
Used in: Strength (M) Core Block 3×15
25

Lying Leg Raise

Core

Lower Abs

On your back — raise straight legs to vertical, lower slowly without arching

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Lie flat, hands under the hips or at the sides for support
  • Keep the legs straight (slight knee bend is fine) and together
  • Raise the legs to vertical using the lower abs
  • Lower slowly — stop before the low back arches off the floor
  • Press the low back down the entire set
  • Too hard? Bend the knees more, or do hanging knee raises if a bar is available
Used in: Strength (M) Core Block 3×12
26

Reverse Crunch

Core

Lower Abs

Curl the hips off the floor, knees toward the chest — control the lower

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Lie on your back, knees bent to ~90°, hands at the sides or under the hips
  • Curl the hips and pelvis up off the floor, bringing the knees toward the chest
  • The movement is the pelvis tilting, not just the legs swinging
  • Lower slowly — don't let the feet crash down
  • Keep it controlled; momentum takes the abs out of it
Used in: Hypertrophy (W) Core Block 3×15
27

Bicycle Crunch

Core

Upper Abs & Obliques

Opposite elbow to opposite knee — slow, full rotation each rep

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Lie on your back, hands lightly behind the head, shoulders lifted off the floor
  • Bring one knee in while rotating the opposite elbow toward it
  • Extend the other leg out straight, just off the floor
  • Alternate sides in a slow, deliberate pedal — not a race
  • Rotate from the torso, don't yank the neck with the hands
  • One of the highest-activation ab exercises when done slowly
Used in: Hypertrophy (W) Core Block 3×20 — Metabolic (F) Core Finisher 20 reps
28

DB Russian Twist

Core

Obliques

Lean back holding a DB — rotate it from hip to hip under control

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Sit with knees bent, lean the torso back to ~45° (feet up if you can balance)
  • Hold one DB with both hands at the chest
  • Rotate the torso to tap the DB toward the floor beside one hip, then the other
  • Move from the waist — the rotation is the work, not arm-swinging
  • Keep the chest tall and back flat, not rounded
  • Count one rep per side or per pair — stay consistent week to week
Used in: Hypertrophy (W) Core Block 3×20
29

DB Side Bend

Core

Obliques

One DB at your side — bend sideways toward it, then pull straight up

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Stand tall, hold one DB in one hand at your side, other hand behind the head
  • Bend sideways toward the DB, lowering it down the leg
  • Pull straight back up by contracting the opposite obliques
  • Move only in the side-to-side plane — no leaning forward or back
  • Finish all reps on one side, then switch the DB to the other hand
  • Use moderate weight; chasing huge loads here can thicken the waist
Used in: Optional oblique accessory — add to any core block
30

Plank

Core

Whole Core (Anti-Extension)

Forearms down, body in a straight line — brace hard and hold

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Forearms on the floor, elbows under the shoulders
  • Body in a straight line from head to heels — no sagging or piking
  • Squeeze the abs, glutes, and quads the entire hold
  • Keep the neck neutral, eyes down at the floor
  • Breathe steadily; don't hold your breath
  • Quality of the brace beats total time — stop when form breaks
Used in: Strength (M) Core Block 3×45s — Metabolic (F) Core Finisher 45s
31

Side Plank

Core

Obliques (Anti-Lateral-Flexion)

On one forearm — hips high, straight line, hold each side

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Lie on your side, elbow under the shoulder, feet stacked or staggered
  • Lift the hips so the body is a straight line, side-on to the floor
  • Drive the bottom hip up — don't let it sag toward the floor
  • Keep the top shoulder stacked over the bottom one
  • Hold for time, then switch sides
  • Carves the obliques and the line along the waist
Used in: Hypertrophy (W) Core Block 3×30s/side
32

V-Up

Core

Full Abs (Upper + Lower)

Reach hands to feet, folding into a "V" — lower with control

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Lie flat, arms extended overhead, legs straight
  • Simultaneously lift the legs and torso, reaching the hands toward the feet
  • Body folds into a "V", balancing on the glutes
  • Lower both halves under control back to the floor
  • Hits the upper and lower abs together — scale to tucked knees if needed
  • Keep the movement smooth, not a jerky snap
Used in: Metabolic (F) Core Finisher 12 reps
33

Flutter Kicks

Core

Lower Abs

Legs straight just off the floor — small fast alternating kicks

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Lie flat, hands under the hips for low-back support
  • Lift both legs a few inches off the floor, straight
  • Kick in small, quick alternating up-and-down motions
  • Keep the low back pressed into the floor the entire set
  • If the back arches, raise the legs higher to ease the leverage
  • Constant tension on the lower abs — for time, not reps
Used in: Metabolic (F) Core Finisher 30s
34

Mountain Climbers

Core / Conditioning

Abs + Cardio

High plank — drive the knees to the chest fast, alternating, hips low

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Start in a high plank, hands under the shoulders, body straight
  • Drive one knee toward the chest, then switch quickly
  • Keep the hips low and level — don't let them pike up
  • Stay on the balls of the feet, core braced throughout
  • The faster the pace, the more it becomes cardio — control the form first
  • Hits the abs while keeping the heart rate high
Used in: Hypertrophy (W) finisher 30s — Metabolic (F) Core Finisher 30s

Conditioning & Finishers

35

DB Thruster

Conditioning

Full Body (Biggest Burn)

Front squat into an overhead press in one fluid motion

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Hold DBs at the shoulders, palms facing in, feet shoulder-width
  • Squat to full depth, chest tall
  • Drive up explosively and use that momentum to press the DBs overhead
  • Lock out overhead, then bring the DBs back to the shoulders
  • One continuous motion squat-to-press — that's what spikes the burn
  • Moderate weight; this is a conditioning movement, not a max lift
Used in: Metabolic (F) Power Primer 3×12
36

DB Swing

Conditioning

Glutes, Hamstrings, Conditioning

Hinge and snap the hips to swing one DB to shoulder height

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Hold one DB by one end with both hands, feet shoulder-width
  • Hinge at the hips (don't squat), letting the DB swing back between the legs
  • Snap the hips forward explosively to drive the DB up to shoulder height
  • The power comes from the glutes and hips, not the arms or shoulders
  • Let the DB float at the top; control it back into the next hinge
  • Keep the back flat and core braced the entire set
Used in: Metabolic (F) Circuit A 15 reps
37

Farmer's Walk

Carry

Grip, Traps, Core, Conditioning

Hold heavy DBs at your sides — walk tall with purpose

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Pick up the heaviest DBs you can carry, one in each hand
  • Stand tall: shoulders back and down, chest up, core braced
  • Walk with controlled steps for the prescribed time
  • Don't lean or shrug excessively — let the traps and grip hold the load
  • Breathe steadily; keep a strong, upright posture throughout
  • Builds a vice grip and a braced midsection while adding conditioning
Used in: Strength (M) Finisher 3×40s
38

Burpee

Conditioning

Full Body (Optional Burnout)

Squat, kick to a plank, push-up, jump the feet in, jump up

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • From standing, squat and place the hands on the floor
  • Kick or step the feet back into a plank
  • Optional push-up, then jump or step the feet back to the hands
  • Stand and jump at the top (or just stand tall to lower impact)
  • Keep the core braced on the plank phase — no sagging hips
  • Step the feet instead of jumping to scale it down
Used in: Metabolic (F) optional finisher 2×10

Travel & Equipment Variations

Band and bodyweight substitutes for every movement above — the versions that run in a hotel room

Band Chest Press

Compound Push

Chest, Front Delts, Triceps

Band across the upper back, a handle in each hand at armpit height

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Run the band across your upper back, under the armpits, one handle in each hand
  • Hands start beside the chest, elbows at about 45° from the torso
  • Press forward and slightly inward until the hands nearly meet at lockout
  • Squeeze the chest hard for a full second at the end of the press
  • Return under control — do not let the band snap the hands back
  • Step further from the anchor, or take a wider stance on the band, to add tension
Used in: Session A (M) and B (W), Bands tier

Band Incline Press

Compound Push

Upper Chest, Front Delts

Band anchored low behind you, press up and forward at roughly 30°

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Anchor the band low, at ankle height in a door hinge
  • Face away from the anchor, handles at chest height, split stance for balance
  • Press up and forward on a 30° line, the same path as an incline dumbbell press
  • Finish with the hands together and slightly above the collarbones
  • Lower slowly and let the band pull the hands back to a stretch across the chest
Used in: Session A (M) and C (F), Bands tier

Band Chest Fly

Isolation

Chest (Stretch and Squeeze)

Band anchored at chest height behind you, arms sweep together in a wide arc

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Anchor at chest height behind you, a handle in each hand, step forward into tension
  • Slight fixed bend in the elbows, arms out wide to start
  • Sweep the hands together in a wide arc, as if hugging a barrel
  • Cross the hands slightly at the finish for an extra squeeze
  • Open slowly to a deep stretch across the chest, then repeat
Used in: Session C (F), Bands tier

Band Seated Row

Compound Pull

Lats, Mid-Back, Rear Delts, Biceps

Band anchored at chest height, row the handles to the ribs

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Anchor the band at chest height, sit or stand facing it, arms extended
  • Start each rep with the shoulder blades allowed to travel forward for a full stretch
  • Pull the handles to the lower ribs, elbows past the torso, wrists neutral
  • Squeeze the shoulder blades together and hold one full second
  • Extend slowly, resisting the band the whole way out
  • For the one-arm version, work one side at a time and let the torso rotate slightly into the stretch
Used in: Sessions A, B and C, Bands tier

Band Lat Pulldown

Compound Pull

Lats (Width)

Band anchored over the top of a door, kneel and pull to the collarbone

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Anchor the band over the top of a closed, locked door, or high in the hinge
  • Kneel or sit facing the door, arms fully extended overhead, slight lean back
  • Pull the handles down to the collarbones by driving the elbows down toward the ribs, not back
  • Think about the lats doing the work and the hands just holding on
  • Squeeze one second at the bottom, then let the arms extend fully overhead again
  • This is the closest thing to a pull-up you can do in a hotel room — it is the reason to travel with bands
Used in: Session B (W) and C (F), Bands tier

Band Overhead Press

Compound Push

Shoulders, Triceps

Stand on the middle of the band, press the handles overhead

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Stand on the centre of the band with both feet, handles at shoulder height
  • Brace the core and squeeze the glutes so the back does not arch
  • Press straight overhead to full lockout, biceps beside the ears
  • Lower under control to shoulder height, keeping tension the whole way
  • Widen the stance on the band to increase resistance
Used in: Session A (M) and C (F), Bands tier

Band Lateral Raise

Isolation

Side Delts (Shoulder Width)

Stand on the band, raise the arms out to shoulder height

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Stand on the band, a handle in each hand at your sides
  • Lead with the elbows, raising the arms out to the sides
  • Stop at shoulder height — no higher, the traps take over above that
  • Pause a beat at the top, lower slowly against the band
  • Keep it light and strict; this is a detail movement, not a strength one
Used in: Sessions B and C, Bands tier

Band Face Pull

Isolation

Rear Delts, Upper Back, Rotator Cuff

Band anchored at eye height, pull to the forehead with high elbows

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Anchor the band at roughly eye height, hold the handles with an overhand grip
  • Step back into tension, arms extended straight out in front
  • Pull the handles toward the forehead, elbows flaring high and wide
  • Finish by rotating the hands back so the knuckles point behind you
  • Squeeze the rear delts and mid-back one second, then extend slowly
  • The best single movement for posture and for balancing all the pressing
Used in: Session B (W), Bands tier

Band Tricep Pushdown

Isolation

Triceps

Band anchored high, elbows pinned, extend to full lockout

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Anchor the band high, over a door or in the top hinge
  • Stand facing the anchor, elbows pinned tight to the ribs, forearms parallel to the floor
  • Extend the elbows fully, driving the hands down and slightly back
  • Squeeze the triceps hard at full lockout for one second
  • Let the forearms return to parallel only — the elbows never leave the ribs
  • For the overhead version, anchor low behind you and extend the arms overhead instead
Used in: Sessions A and C, Bands tier

Band Curl

Isolation

Biceps, Forearms

Stand on the band, curl the handles to the shoulders

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Stand on the centre of the band, handles at your sides, palms forward
  • Elbows stay pinned to the ribs the whole set
  • Curl to the shoulders, squeeze one second at the top
  • Lower slowly — band tension is highest at the top, so fight it on the way down
  • For the hammer version, hold the handles with palms facing each other throughout
Used in: Sessions A, B and C, Bands tier

Band Crunch

Flexion

Abs (Loaded Flexion)

Band anchored high behind you, crunch the ribs toward the hips

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Anchor the band high behind you and kneel facing away from it
  • Hold the handles beside the head, elbows pointing forward
  • Crunch by pulling the ribs down toward the hips — the hips do not move
  • Squeeze the abs hard at the bottom, then return slowly under tension
  • This is loaded ab work, which is what actually builds the muscle. Twelve to fifteen hard reps beats a hundred easy ones
Used in: Session A (M), Bands tier

Decline Push-Up

Compound Push

Upper Chest, Front Delts, Triceps

Feet on the bed or a chair, hands on the floor

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Feet elevated on the bed, a chair, or the edge of the bath — the higher the feet, the harder it is
  • Hands slightly wider than shoulders, body in one straight line from head to heels
  • Lower for a full three seconds until the chest touches the floor
  • Press up explosively, squeezing the chest at the top
  • Elevating the feet shifts the load to the upper chest and shoulders, which is exactly the incline press substitute
  • If twelve becomes easy, raise the feet higher or slow the descent to five seconds
Used in: Session A (M), No Equipment tier

Wide & Diamond Push-Up

Compound Push

Chest (Wide) / Triceps (Diamond)

Two hand positions, two very different jobs

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Wide: hands well outside the shoulders, elbows flare to about 60°, chest to the floor. Maximum chest stretch
  • Diamond: hands together under the chest, index fingers and thumbs forming a triangle, elbows tucked tight to the ribs
  • The diamond version is the best bodyweight triceps builder there is
  • Both: body in one line, three seconds down, no sagging hips
  • Elevate the feet on either version when the reps get easy
Used in: Sessions B and C, No Equipment tier

Diamond Push-Up

Isolation

Triceps, Inner Chest

Hands in a diamond under the chest, elbows tucked

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Hands together directly under the chest, index fingers and thumbs touching to form a diamond
  • Elbows stay tucked close to the ribs throughout — do not let them flare
  • Lower until the chest touches the hands, three seconds down
  • Press to full lockout and squeeze the triceps
  • Drop to the knees to keep good form rather than cutting the range short
Used in: Sessions B and C, No Equipment tier

Pike Push-Up

Compound Push

Shoulders, Triceps

Hips high, feet elevated, head lowers between the hands

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Feet on the bed, hands on the floor, hips driven high so the body makes an inverted V
  • The closer to vertical you get the hips, the more it becomes a true overhead press
  • Lower the crown of the head toward the floor between the hands
  • Press back to full lockout, pushing the head through the arms at the top
  • This is the overhead press substitute. Nothing else with zero equipment loads the delts like it
  • Progress by walking the feet higher up the bed or headboard
Used in: Sessions A and C, No Equipment tier

Door-Handle Row

Compound Pull

Lats, Mid-Back, Biceps

Grip both handles of a solid door, lean back, row your chest to the door

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Use a solid, fully open door. Throw a folded towel over the top edge so it cannot swing
  • Stand facing the door, grip both handles, feet either side of the base, toes close to the door
  • Walk the feet forward and lean back until the arms are straight and you feel the stretch
  • Row your chest toward the door, driving the elbows back and squeezing the shoulder blades
  • Pause one second at the top, then lower slowly to a full stretch
  • Walk the feet further forward, or lift one foot, to make it harder
  • This is your best zero-equipment back movement. Do not rush it
Used in: Sessions A, B and C, No Equipment tier

Towel Row (One Arm)

Compound Pull

Lats, Biceps

Towel looped round a door handle, one hand, lean back and pull

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Loop a bath towel round a solid door handle and grip both ends in one hand
  • Feet close to the door, lean back until the arm is straight
  • Pull your torso in toward the hand, driving the elbow past the ribs
  • Let the shoulder blade travel forward at the bottom for a real stretch
  • The further you walk the feet forward, the more bodyweight you are lifting
  • Work one arm at a time so each side gets the full load
Used in: Sessions A and B, No Equipment tier

Sliding Lat Pullover

Compound Pull

Lats (Long Stretch)

Kneel, hands on a towel, slide it out overhead and pull it back

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Kneel on a soft surface, place both hands on a folded towel on a smooth floor
  • Keep the arms straight with a fixed slight bend at the elbow
  • Slide the towel forward and overhead, sinking the chest toward the floor until the lats stretch hard
  • Pull the towel back toward the knees using the lats, not the arms or the lower back
  • Keep the ribs down and the core braced so the lower back never arches
  • Go only as far as you can control — this loads the lats through a range nothing else reaches without equipment
Used in: Sessions B and C, No Equipment tier

Prone Y and T Raise

Isolation

Rear Delts, Lower Traps, Mid-Back

Face down on the floor, lift the arms against gravity

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Lie face down, forehead resting on a folded towel
  • T raise: arms straight out to the sides at 90°, thumbs up. Lift, squeeze one second, lower slowly
  • Y raise: arms overhead at about 45°, thumbs up. Same lift, squeeze, slow lower
  • The range is small and the load is light — the whole point is the one-second squeeze at the top
  • Keep the neck neutral and the ribs on the floor. No arching the lower back to cheat height
  • Hold a shoe, a water bottle, or a full toiletry bag in each hand once fifteen reps get easy
Used in: Sessions B and C, No Equipment tier

Bed-Edge Dip

Isolation

Triceps, Front Delts

Hands on the bed edge behind you, feet out front, lower and press

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Sit on the edge of the bed or a solid chair, hands beside the hips gripping the edge
  • Slide the hips forward off the edge, legs extended out in front
  • Lower by bending the elbows straight back — never let them flare out to the sides
  • Go to about 90° at the elbow, then press back to lockout and squeeze the triceps
  • Bend the knees to make it easier; put the heels on a chair to make it much harder
  • Stop short if you feel it in the front of the shoulder rather than the triceps
Used in: Session A (M), No Equipment tier

Towel Curl (Self-Resisted)

Isolation

Biceps, Forearms

Towel under one foot, curl against your own downward pull

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Stand on one end of a towel, hold the other end in one hand, palm up
  • Pull down through the foot while curling up with the arm, fighting yourself
  • Take a full four seconds to curl up and four seconds to lower
  • The tension is entirely under your control, so make it genuinely hard for all twelve reps
  • Isometric and self-resisted work will not build arms like dumbbells will — it will hold what you have across a travel week
Used in: Sessions A, B and C, No Equipment tier

Hollow Body Hold

Isometric

Abs (Full Anterior Chain)

Low back pinned to the floor, arms and legs long

Form Cues

  • Lie on your back, press the lower back flat into the floor and keep it there
  • Lift the shoulder blades and the legs off the floor, arms reaching back past the ears
  • The lower the arms and legs, the harder it is — the lower back must stay flat
  • If the back lifts even slightly, bend the knees or raise the legs higher until it stays down
  • Breathe shallowly and hold. Shaking is the point
  • Far more effective than crunches for the deep abdominal wall

Full stretch at the bottom, one-second squeeze at the top

  • Let the heels drop for a full stretch at the bottom
  • Rise as high onto the toes as you can and hold one full second
  • Lower slowly — the calves respond to the stretch and the pause, not to bouncing

Close-Grip DB Press

Compound Push

Triceps & Inner Chest

Dumbbells touching, elbows tucked, press from the chest

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Keep the DBs pressed against each other for the whole set — that is what shifts the work to the triceps
  • Elbows stay tucked close to the ribs, not flared out to the sides
  • Lower to the lower chest under control, then press straight up to lockout
  • Squeeze the triceps hard at the top of every rep
  • This is the heavy triceps movement the program was missing — skull crushers and extensions load the long head, this loads the whole muscle under real weight
  • On the floor version the upper arms touch down, which gives a natural pause and protects the shoulder

Kettlebell-Specific Movements

The four movements on the kettlebell tier that have no direct dumbbell equivalent — every other KB movement follows the form cues of its dumbbell counterpart above

Kettlebell Swing

Power / Hinge

Glutes, Hamstrings, Posterior Chain

Hip snap, not a squat and not a front raise

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Stand with the bell a foot in front of you, hinge and hike it back behind the knees like a rugby pass
  • Snap the hips forward hard and stand tall — the bell floats up on momentum, the arms do nothing
  • Squeeze the glutes and brace the abs at the top. The bell stops at chest height, not overhead
  • Let it fall, catch the load with the hips by hinging back, and go straight into the next rep
  • This is a hip hinge, not a squat. The knees bend a little; the hips move a lot
  • Keep the spine neutral throughout. If the lower back rounds at the bottom, the hinge is too shallow
  • It sits in the Legs session as the hip-extension movement, in the slot a glute bridge takes on other tiers
Used in: Legs (Sat), Kettlebell tier

KB High Pull

Compound Pull

Rear Delts, Upper Back, Traps

A swing that finishes with the elbow driving high and wide

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Start exactly as a swing: hinge, hike, hip snap
  • As the bell reaches chest height, drive the elbow high and back, pulling the bell toward the shoulder
  • The elbow stays above the wrist and flares out to the side, which is what loads the rear delts
  • Reverse the path and let the bell drop straight back into the hinge
  • Power comes from the hips. The pull finishes the movement, it does not start it
  • This replaces the bent-over rear delt fly on the kettlebell tier, which is why it sits in Session B and C
Used in: Sessions B (W) and C (F), Kettlebell tier

Half-Kneeling KB Press

Compound Push

Shoulders, Triceps, Core

One knee down, one bell overhead, no cheating with the legs

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Half-kneeling: one knee down, the other foot forward, both at 90°
  • Press the bell on the same side as the down knee — that is the position that forces the core to work
  • Rack the bell on the back of the forearm, elbow tight to the ribs to start
  • Press straight overhead to full lockout, biceps beside the ear
  • Squeeze the glute of the down leg the whole set. It stops the lower back arching
  • The kneeling position removes any leg drive, so the shoulder gets all of the work at a lighter load
  • Go bottoms-up (bell inverted, handle gripped hard) when you want it much harder without a heavier bell
Used in: Session C (F), Kettlebell tier

KB Deficit Push-Up

Compound Push

Chest (Deep Stretch)

Hands on the bell handles so the chest drops below them

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Set two bells shoulder-width apart, handles running the same direction as your body
  • Grip the handles and get into a push-up position. Press down hard so the bells cannot tip
  • Lower until the chest drops below the level of the handles — that extra range is the whole point
  • The deficit gives a deeper chest stretch than a floor push-up, and stretch is where the growth signal is strongest
  • Neutral grip is also easier on the wrists than a flat-palm push-up
  • Elevate the feet on a bench or bed to add load once the reps get easy
  • Use bells heavy enough that they will not slide. Test with bodyweight before committing
Used in: Session C (F), Kettlebell tier

Complete-Coverage Additions

The movements added in v3 so that every muscle gets two complementary exercises in every session

V1

DB Shrug

v3

Upper Traps

Straight up and down — no rolling, no bouncing

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Stand tall, dumbbells hanging at your sides, arms straight and stay straight
  • Shrug the shoulders straight up toward the ears, nothing else moves
  • Hold the top for a full second. That second is the whole exercise
  • Lower slowly and let the traps stretch at the bottom before the next rep
  • Never roll the shoulders. Rolling adds rotation the joint does not need and takes load off the traps
  • Go heavier than feels dignified. Traps handle load well and the range is short
  • Bands tier: stand on the band, same movement. No equipment: hold a packed bag in each hand
V2

Wide-Grip Upright Row

v3

Traps & Side Delts

Wide grip, elbows stop at shoulder height

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Hold the dumbbells in front of the thighs, hands wider than shoulder width
  • Pull the elbows up and out to the sides, leading with the elbows not the hands
  • Stop when the elbows reach shoulder height. Going higher is what irritates shoulders
  • The wide grip is not optional — a narrow grip is the version that causes impingement
  • Lower under control, feel the traps and side delts lengthen
  • If it pinches at any height, drop to the Y raise instead and skip this one
V3

Wide-Elbow Row

v3

Upper Back Width

Elbows flared to 90°, pull to the sternum

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Hinge forward to roughly 45°, or lie chest-down on an incline bench
  • Let the arms hang and the shoulder blades spread fully at the bottom
  • Pull with the elbows wide, not tucked, driving them out and back
  • Aim the hands at the sternum, not the hip. The hip path builds thickness, this path builds width
  • Squeeze the shoulder blades together for a second at the top
  • Wide elbows shift the work from the lats to the mid traps and rear delts, which is the point
V4

Incline DB Fly

v3

Upper Chest

Low to high, wide arc, deep stretch

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Set the bench to about 30°. Higher than that and it becomes a delt exercise
  • Start with the dumbbells over the chest, elbows softly bent and locked at that angle
  • Open the arms out and down in a wide arc until you feel a real stretch across the upper chest
  • Bring them back along the same arc, finishing with the dumbbells close but not touching
  • The elbow angle never changes. If it is opening and closing, you are pressing
  • The stretched bottom position is where the growth happens, so do not cut it short
  • No bench: lie on the floor and stop where the upper arms touch down
V5

Squeeze Press

v3

Inner & Mid Chest

Press the dumbbells hard together for the entire set

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Hold two dumbbells touching each other over the chest, palms facing in
  • Press them together hard, as if trying to crush something between them, and keep pressing
  • Lower to the chest and press back up while never letting the contact go
  • The tension comes from the squeeze, not the weight, so lighter dumbbells work fine here
  • Hexagonal dumbbells are easier to keep together than round ones
  • This keeps constant tension on the chest, including at lockout where a normal press unloads
V6

Deficit Push-Up

v3

Chest (Stretched)

Hands elevated so the chest drops below them

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Set hands on two dumbbells, two books, or the edges of two chairs
  • Take a normal push-up position with the hands on those raised surfaces
  • Lower until the chest drops below the level of your hands. That extra depth is the whole point
  • Pause for a second at the bottom, in the stretch, then press up
  • Elbows at roughly 45° to the body, not flared to 90°
  • Feet on a bed or chair makes it harder; knees down makes it easier
V7

Archer Push-Up

v3

Chest (Unilateral)

One arm bends, the other stays straight

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Take a wider than normal push-up stance
  • Lower toward one hand, bending that elbow while the other arm straightens out to the side
  • Push back up through the bent arm. That side is doing most of the work
  • Alternate sides each rep, or do all reps on one side then switch
  • This is the closest a hotel room gets to a heavy one-arm press
  • Too hard? Keep more weight on the straight arm until you build up
V8

Concentration Curl

v3

Biceps (Peak Contraction)

Elbow braced on the inner thigh, no swing possible

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Sit, lean forward, and brace the back of your upper arm against the inside of your thigh
  • Let the arm hang straight down with the dumbbell
  • Curl up slowly, supinating so the palm faces the shoulder at the top
  • Squeeze hard for a full second at the top. The squeeze is why this exercise is in the program
  • Lower all the way to a straight arm every rep
  • The braced elbow makes cheating impossible, which is exactly what you want at the end of an arm block
V9

Spider Curl

v3

Biceps (No Momentum)

Chest supported, arms hanging straight down

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Lie chest-down on an incline bench, or stand and lean your chest against something solid
  • Let both arms hang straight down, perpendicular to the floor
  • Curl up without letting the upper arms drift forward or back
  • Squeeze at the top, then lower until the arms are completely straight
  • With the arms hanging in front of the body the biceps get no help from the front delts
  • Body-swing is physically impossible here, so the weight will be lighter than a standing curl. That is correct
V10

Reverse Curl

v3

Brachioradialis & Forearm Extensors

Palms down for the whole rep

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Stand with dumbbells at the thighs, palms facing backward (overhand grip)
  • Curl up keeping the palms facing down the entire way
  • Wrists stay straight and firm — do not let them curl or flop
  • Elbows stay pinned to your sides
  • This builds the brachioradialis, the muscle that gives the forearm its width just below the elbow
  • It also thickens the top of the forearm, which the wrist curl does not touch
  • Expect to use noticeably less weight than a normal curl
V11

Wrist Curl & Reverse Wrist Curl

v3

Forearm Flexors & Extensors

Forearms braced, only the wrists move

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Sit and rest the forearms along the thighs, wrists hanging past the knees
  • Wrist curl (palms up): let the dumbbell roll down to the fingertips, then curl it back and squeeze
  • Reverse wrist curl (palms down): lift the back of the hand toward you, small range, slow
  • The forearms never leave the thighs. If they lift, you are using the biceps
  • Full finger roll on the palms-up version is what makes it work; a half rep does almost nothing
  • High reps here. Forearms are endurance muscle and respond to 15-25
  • Do the palms-up version first, palms-down second. The extensors are much weaker
V12

Towel Wring

v3

Forearms (No Equipment)

Wring a towel as hard as you can, both directions

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Take a hand towel and grip one end in each hand
  • Wring it in opposite directions as hard as you can and hold the tension
  • Flexor version: wring inward, curling the fingers and wrists down
  • Extensor version: wring outward, driving the backs of the hands away from each other
  • Work for time, roughly 30-45 seconds per set, not for reps
  • Wet the towel to make it harder and to give the grip something to fight
  • This is the hotel-room substitute for wrist curls. It is not as good, but it is not nothing
V13

Tricep Kickback

v3

Triceps (Lateral Head)

Upper arm locked parallel to the floor

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Hinge forward until the torso is close to parallel with the floor
  • Pin the upper arm against your side, parallel to the floor. It does not move again
  • Straighten the elbow until the whole arm is locked out behind you
  • Squeeze hard at lockout for a full second, then lower under control
  • If the upper arm drops or swings, the weight is too heavy. This is a light exercise
  • It loads the triceps hardest at full contraction, which is the opposite of the overhead extension. That is why they are paired
V14

Sliding Tricep Extension

v3

Triceps (No Equipment)

Forearms on a towel, slide out and back

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Kneel with forearms on a towel on a smooth floor, or hands on a towel on a table
  • Keeping the upper arms still, slide the forearms forward until the arms are extended overhead
  • Your body lowers toward the floor as the arms extend. Keep the hips in line, do not sag
  • Drag the forearms back in by straightening the elbows. Triceps only, no hip push
  • Slide less far to make it easier; more far to make it brutal
  • This is the bodyweight version of a skull crusher and it loads the long head under stretch
V15

Sliding Chest Fly

v3

Chest (No Equipment)

Hands on towels, open the arms wide

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Push-up position with a towel or sock under each hand on a smooth floor
  • Keep a soft, fixed bend in the elbows
  • Slide the hands out to the sides in a wide arc until the chest is stretched near the floor
  • Pull them back together under the chest. The chest does the work, not the hands
  • Go only as wide as you can pull back from. This gets hard fast
  • Knees down shortens the lever if the full version is too much
V16

Sliding Leg Curl

v3

Hamstrings (Knee Flexion)

Heels on a towel, hips stay high

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Lie on your back, heels on a towel or sliders on a smooth floor
  • Bridge the hips up and keep them up for the entire set. Do not let them drop
  • Slide the heels out until the legs are nearly straight
  • Drag the heels back in toward the hips by bending the knees
  • The hamstrings work through knee flexion here, which the Romanian deadlift never trains
  • Hips dropping is the giveaway that you are tired. Stop the set when they will not stay up
  • Band tier: anchor a band at the ankles and curl against it, lying face down
V17

Sissy Squat

v3

Quads (Stretched)

Knees travel forward, torso and thighs in one line

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Hold something for balance. A doorframe or the back of a chair
  • Rise onto the balls of the feet and let the knees travel forward
  • Lean back as the knees bend, keeping the hips, torso and thighs in a straight line
  • Go down as far as the knees allow without pain, then drive back up
  • This puts the quads under a deep stretch that a normal squat never reaches
  • Start with a very small range. Knees need time to adapt to this one
  • Skip it entirely if you have any knee pain and do more split squats instead
V18

Step-Up

v3

Glutes & Quads

High step, no push off the trailing foot

Watch Tutorial

Form Cues

  • Use a step high enough that the working thigh starts parallel to the floor or lower
  • Plant the whole foot on the step, weight through the heel
  • Drive up entirely through the front leg. The back foot does not push
  • Stand fully tall at the top and squeeze the glute
  • Lower slowly under control. Do not just drop off the step
  • The higher the step, the more glute. A low step turns it into a calf-and-quad move
  • All reps on one leg, then switch, so the working glute stays under tension
